Measurements and limits

The egg tip starts at (0, 0), 8 cm ahead of the front axle and 55 cm ahead of the rear axle. The axle spacing is 47 cm front-to-rear, 18 cm between front wheels, and 19.5 cm between rear wheels. The body points from the starting egg-tip position directly to the aiming box on perpendicular halfway line L1. Its heading changes continuously as the egg tip travels along a circular arc. The selected front-axle angle stays fixed relative to the body. Positive front-axle angle turns right; positive aiming-box and X distances point left. The egg-tip turn radius is calibrated from your two supplied trajectories, assuming their coordinates are centimetres and track the egg tip: 3.18 m at 7° and 1.61 m at 15°. Angles outside the measured 7°–15° range are estimates. The suggested settings use a circle through the egg start, X, and target; they are exact only within this model. They are unavailable when X is at least half the total distance from L2, since that would require a backward or sideways launch. The outside can's inner edge is 100 cm left of C; the second can is placed so X is halfway between can centers. The practice FAIL flag still uses the egg tip's crossing of L1. The simulated vehicle travels to its first crossing of L3, the line through the target parallel to L1. If the circular path cannot reach L3, the graph shows its farthest-forward point and no numeric score. The can bonus uses a 7.5 cm can diameter and an approximate 22 cm-wide rectangular vehicle footprint; actual parts may differ. Surface grip, wheel scrub, barriers, speed changes, braking delay, and penalties are not included. These results are geometric estimates, not competition scores.

Calibrate from real runs

For each successful practice run, enter the actual knob readings that made the egg pass through X on L1 and stop at the target. X must be the egg's measured crossing position, not just the intended can position. The three suggested X and distance pairs below are starting points for experiments; replace them with your real results. Speed is not needed to fit the path.
